<span class="dropcap">A</span> decade ago, while I was trying to set up a website for my family. I could not recall how to set the margin and padding properties for an HTML element. I went crazy for a moment and then searched online to define left and right values the right way. What a trouble for me that day!

## Code is poetry.
After setting up the values, with a troubled face, I looked at the computer screen for a while. I do not want to go through this trouble again; I said to myself. My staring at margin properties, right into the code editor, had not stopped yet. In a moment, I noticed four alphabets popping out of the code and dancing on the screen. They were T, R, B, and L.

And, that sounds like **TR**ou**BL**e, doesn't it?

I made a [mnemonic](https://art19.com/shows/merriam-websters-word-of-the-day/episodes/a883909f-cfb7-4bf5-ab14-07a9f940597f)<sup>1</sup> and a haiku, right away. Code is like poetry. 🤪

> Oh TRBL!  
> Dear TRBL,  
> Never make me forget,  
> Top, Right, Bottom, Left, ever again  
> Out of trouble,
> Scremaed the poor programmer.
> 😱  
> 
> <cite>by: Abdul-Jabbar Bozdar</cite>  

Following this, both margin and padding never made me anxious again. But till today, I cannot recall which value is first and the last or in the middle. Unless I reconstruct the abbreviation TRBL as mnemonic while applying margin and padding. It simply becomes Top, Right, Bottom, and Left for me.

```css
/* when TRBL is TRouBLe. */
.class element {
    margin: top right bottom left;
    padding: T R B L;
    }
```

## Why Blog About This?
A few days back, I was creating front matter data for my blog pages. I forgot the sequence of variables in different layouts. Although it didn't matter, I wanted to be consistent with a pattern. Again, I went through for an upset moment, but thank God, it was short-lived. Three abbreviations popped on screen and became mnemonics for the poor coder.

* LTS (Ubuntu): Layout, Title, Slug
* LTD (company): Layout, Title, Description
* LTE (4g): Layout, Title, Excerpt

It's not only me who does these things. This trick dates back to my college and school times. I remember, we were experimenting in an electronics lab once during the first semester. One instructor came toward my group. He said, "if it is difficult for you to remember resistor<sup>2</sup> colour codes then you better create mnemonics." It would be easier for you to recall them throughout the life.

He went on telling us how to remember [resistor colour codes](https://www.allaboutcircuits.com/tools/resistor-color-code-calculator/) as '**BB ROYG BV G W**'. I never forgot this ever again.

## Now Never Forget
Mnemonics are always helpful. They are fun to make and always pop up at the right time. They can be your cheat sheet in your long-term memory. Whatever is funnier and easier improves endorphin and dopamine. Therefore, this helps to memorize difficult forever. I will write about making patterns some other day then.


1. **Mnemonic** is pronounced as 'ni ma nik' which means to intend to assist in memory.
2. **Resistor** is a passive electronic component to block or reduce the flow of voltage.
