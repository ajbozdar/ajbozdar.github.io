<span class="dropcap">W</span>hen I first learned [about the internet]({{site.url}}/amazing-internet/), I knew only one browser. It was the Internet Explorer. There was another which I never used, the Netscape navigator. It was December 2004 when I was visiting a computer tutoring centre with a class fellow. We saw our instructor running a new software to browse the internet. I asked him, what's that? It's a new browser, he replied; faster than the explorer, he further said.

In a few days, all the students got the new browser on their respective computers. Everybody was happy. Some continued using explorer, but I long forgotten that blue thing.

## The Best Use of Internet Explorer
When I got my own PC at home, we launched the explorer only once, and it was to download the Mozilla Firefox. Later on came the Chrome with its all due controversies. Chrome won the hearts because everything was right in there for them. Their search engine, email, and everything else that existed then.

I continued using Mozilla. As Firefox showed signs of fatigue, I switched to the Maxthon browser for a little time and then back to Firefox. Chrome was still there. It couldn't shine in front of me.

<div class = "note blacknote">
  <p>All the alternate browser developers themselves look for the Google money, including Mozilla. They also do not have anybody else to look for. 👀</p>
</div>

After 2011, privacy issues started buzzing on the internet, and Yahoo was hacked. Masses began returning to Firefox, but the Mozilla developers couldn't keep them. They had their own problems. I saw some people using Opera, but it never worked for me.

![google browser engine or google money.webp]({{site.url}}/images/google-browser-engine-google-money.webp){:loading="lazy" width="800" height="auto"}_Either you are using Google's engine or Google's money. 🤷‍♂️_

## The Default Browser of the Free World
Now that the world has come to know, Google is a monopoly. The company dictates how the internet should be. Google chooses what services show up on their platforms and what cannot. People's daily lives are well integrated with Google services because of Android. It is hard to say that we can live apart. Either the company splits or not. 

<div class ="note rednote">
<p>One way or another, we will keep choosing G services because there is no alternate for now. But, Google is not the internet.</p>
</div>

If any company starts something new, Google offers them research and development funds. In return, Google asks them to make their search engine the default engine for the users. Once the R&D money is in the pockets of the team leads, it gets difficult to make the original manifesto a reality.

![Epiphany Gnome browser running on Fedora 40]({{site.url}}/images/gnome-browser.webp){:loading="lazy" width="800" height="auto"}_Epiphany: The Gnome browser running on Fedora 40_

<div class = "note yellownote">
<small><strong>What does court verdict mean against Google?</strong></small>  
  <p>Nothing! The court verdicts leave zero impact on Google, or the people who run the company.</p> 
</div>

The only thing one can do now is to keep track of their online record. How families are accessing things online and create safe zones for kids. Google has recently blocked the uBlock extension. This was not a fair decision, I would say. Either it is Lavender or the monopoly in the market, Google makes decisions that are not in favour of the masses.

## Light at the End of the Tunnel
I have never used Chrome, the default browser of the world. I would keep using the minor alternatives and the de-googled software as much as possible. Among them are the Vivaldi, Firefox, and Epiphany. There is some hope in [Ladybird](https://ladybird.org). But, the disparity roams free for now.
