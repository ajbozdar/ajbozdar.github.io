<span class="dropcap">C</span>anonical has decided to discontinue the [Unity shell]({{site.url}}/ubuntu-unity-desktop/) in favour of Gnome, and it has also begun transitioning to snaps. Snapd is a new packaging system. I am afraid I won't find up to date `.deb` packages in near future. 

I found Canonical's decisions quite disheartening. They first introduced the Ubuntu One service and discontinued it as soon as Ubuntu users got accustomed to it. Now, the Unity shell is going away when it just matured enough to become a complete desktop environment on its own.

I thought of transition as well from Ubuntu to a different distribution. I tried Fedora for that reason, but it seems I am not ready. Fedora has first class support for Gnome, but there seems some intel and Nvidia drivers problem on my Dell. Canonical will release 18.04 LTS in six months. I will see how Ubuntu accommodates Gnome. 

Ubuntu has shipped a customised Gnome in 17.10 already, whereas Fedora offers Gnome as it is. 

## Trying Fedora 26 in VirtualBox

![Fedora v26 Overview]({{site.url}}/images/fedora-26-overview.webp)_Gnome Overview Pane in Fedora 26_

I allocated 2 GiB RAM, 2 Cores, and 8 GiB of disk for the Fedora VM. These configs were good enough to test Fedora. I liked the install process. The Anaconda installer was intuitive. The overall installation process was simple. Fedora did not allow me to create a short password. It forced me to use an alphanumeric strong password of eight characters at least.

![Fedora Anaconda Installation Process]({{site.url}}/images/fedora-26-anaconda-installation-process.webp)_Fedora Anaconda Installer in Action_

The partitioning process was also easier and similar to Ubuntu. Fedora installed fairly newer versions of default apps, including the Libre Office suite.

![Fedora Apps Overiew]({{site.url}}/images/fedora-26-apps-overview.webp)_The Gnome dashboard overview on Fedora 26_

Overall, my experience was positive, but I had trouble with a few drivers, so I would keep using Ubuntu for a while. I might move to Fedora when I buy an AMD computer.
