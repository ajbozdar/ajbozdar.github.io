We were having some relaxed time, but that did not last long enough. He began interviewing students. He asked questions like where are you from, and what does your father do for a living?

> The blog post title comes from a fellow blogger, [Hyde](https://lazybear.io/posts/re-my-dad-kicked-bombs-for-a-living/) from France, who first shared a similar anecdote on his website.

## My Father is a Cook.
He began questioning nearly everybody. What does your father do? He is a cook, sir. That's why you are so fat, he replied with a fume. You must be eating half a dozen loaves of bread daily, he went on saying. Everybody laughed at him. The fat boy lived on, thrived, and now raising his family.

Now was my turn. He looked at me and asked the same question. I answered, "my father is a sub-engineer and works at the power house." There was one power house in my area. He made a face and moved on. Sub-engineers were more of operators rather than executives. Neither they had any luxuries in life nor great incentives at work. He used to go for the field visits and returned very late. I used to stare at the wall clock and counted the time tick by tick.

## He Never Compromised on Education.
I often saw him studying at night until I reached class 5. He persuaded his undergraduate studies while working full time. The day he got his well deserving promotion as an engineering manager, I could see our relatives come and congratulate him. He was first in the family to reach that level of professional success. He made to the highest. Yet, he never took the pride. He never believed if he was the first and the last one in the family to become successful. For him, excellent education was everything, and dad proved it. He encouraged and also paved the way to success for many ungrateful relatives. He advocated education for everybody on top of everything. It was my dad who taught me how to [navigate through a website]({{site.url}}/amazing-internet/) and helped me to create my first email address on yahoo.

## Then Came the Scholarship
When he was promoted, people around us also changed. They gradually became closer to us than before. I was not sure what people wanted, but they were never happy with my parents. My parents were paying more attention to us in early 2000.

As soon as I finished junior high school, I got a scholarship to study in a different senior high school than the one in my city. He was delighted and began preparing to send me.

I could never find out what happened as he asked me to withdraw the scholarship and study in the local senior high school where all my class fellows were going.

## Clash of Clans
Those were the rough days. I did not want to attend a local school. I even wrote an acceptance letter on my own to the principal of the high school, and of course I would not get a reply.

Time evolved, and I became a tax paying adult myself. I observed they were his friends who made him fear if I lived in a different city, I could end up in something wrong. He bought that fear and made his decision that I never expected.

Nowadays, when I recall all this, I dwell on events. It would have been different for me if he had written the acceptance letter for me. But we all know more is less, and it is never enough. For me and my siblings, he invested his all resources to make sure that we had a good education. Now we all have 16 and 18 years of education. We can't be happier than this, and we are utmost grateful for everything he and mom did for us.

## If He had Kicked the Bombs!
Today when I was buying my dad's bisoprolol, a thought came to my mind. Many people are no less than the bombs who, out of greed, make sure that others never progress or live a happy life at all. Whatever dad did, he did right anyway. Now we want him to relax, be healthy, and be happy. He himself struggled a lot.

The word bomb sounds like to the word 'bum' in the English language, so I could only wish my dad if he had kicked those bombs and allowed me to take the scholarship.
